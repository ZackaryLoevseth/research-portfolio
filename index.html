The workflow

A result is more than an answer.

Select a step to see what I direct, where AI helps, and what has to survive checking. This explains the workflow; it is not a live benchmark.

Keep the target explicit.

I define the question, assumptions, and stopping conditions. AI helps map possible approaches. A nearby, easier problem is not silently substituted for the original one.

Evidence gate: a written target and an explicit account of what would count as a result.

  1. Question. Define the target, assumptions, and stopping conditions.
  2. Explore. Use AI for possible arguments, literature leads, and code; treat outputs as candidates.
  3. Check. Inspect proof obligations, run reproducible checks, and test whether deliberate errors are rejected.
  4. Correct. Reopen dependent claims when evidence changes; preserve unaffected results.
  5. Release. Publish a versioned argument and evidence package with explicit limitations and AI-use disclosure.

The question behind it

Can we help each other notice what we miss?

My working research program, Epistemic Transition Integrity (ETI), asks how people and AI can preserve evidence, correct dependent claims, and avoid mistaking a plausible story for a result.

The wider idea is that our assumptions can limit what we notice—and that a collaborator can sometimes help reveal them. That is a research hypothesis, not a demonstrated law about every mind or proof that human–AI teams always perform better.
