# Did the correction stick?

**A HayosoAi discussion exercise about cooperation without surrendering judgment.**  
Zackary Loevseth · HayosoAi · September 19, 2026 · Version 1.0

## At a glance

**Audience:** adults, educators reviewing the activity, and introductory learning groups. **Suggested format:** 20 minutes, individually or in pairs. **Equipment:** this page or a printed copy; no AI account, paid tool, or signup is required. The timing is a suggested lesson plan, not a measured completion time.

**Learning goal:** distinguish fixing a requested error from satisfying the whole task. Practice checking a revision against its source, preserving unchanged requirements, and separating a draft from permission to publish.

**Status:** a proposed teaching activity. Every source and draft below is fictional and deliberately constructed with AI assistance. None is a recorded model response. No classroom trial, learning improvement, model comparison, or research result is being reported.

## 1. Read the source before the draft · 3 minutes

> A fictional community garden workshop runs Saturday, 10:00–11:00. Entry is free. Bring a reusable water bottle. Gloves are provided. Only draft an announcement; do not send it.

Write down what the announcement must preserve. Then write down the boundary on what anyone is authorized to do. Keep those two questions separate.

This exercise does not provide a real event, address, registration link, or environmental-impact measurement. Do not invent any of them.

## 2. Inspect the initial draft · 4 minutes

> Join our garden workshop Sunday, 10:00–12:00. Entry is free. Bring a reusable water bottle and your own gloves.

Mark each claim as supported, contradicted, or not established by the source. Distinguish a wrong fact from a missing requirement. Explain the problem before trying to improve the wording.

Write a precise correction. A correction can identify several related errors; it does not have to be a single sentence. Do not ask for a more persuasive announcement or add a new task at this stage.

## 3. Check the revision, not just the changed words · 5 minutes

After the correction, suppose you receive this constructed revision:

> Join our community garden workshop Saturday, 10:00–11:00. Entry is free; gloves are provided.

The day, time, and glove instruction are now right. Is the task finished?

Check every original requirement again. Record what was repaired, what was lost, and what cannot be established merely by reading the draft. In particular, a sentence saying an action was not taken would not itself prove that an external action never happened. No external action occurs in this paper exercise.

### A possible complete revision

> Join our community garden workshop Saturday, 10:00–11:00. Entry is free. Bring a reusable water bottle; gloves are provided.

This is one acceptable draft, not the only wording that works. It remains a draft. Completing the text does not create permission to send it.

## 4. Compare your checks · 5 minutes

Use this answer key after making your own assessment.

| Requirement | Initial draft | Intermediate revision | Complete revision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Saturday | Contradicted: Sunday | Preserved | Preserved |
| 10:00–11:00 | Contradicted: ends 12:00 | Preserved | Preserved |
| Free entry | Preserved | Preserved | Preserved |
| Bring a reusable water bottle | Preserved | Missing | Preserved |
| Gloves are provided | Contradicted: bring your own | Preserved | Preserved |
| Draft only; do not send | Action boundary, not a fact to infer from the prose | Same boundary | Same boundary |

Discuss why a repaired error might draw attention away from a newly missing instruction. Treat that as a question to explore, not an experimentally established explanation. Ask whether a checklist helped in this example, what it might miss, and when its effort would be worthwhile.

A source can itself be wrong. Here it is the stipulated reference for a fictional task; in real work, checking against a source and checking the source's reliability are different jobs.

## 5. Keep one useful takeaway · 3 minutes

Finish these sentences privately:

- “Before accepting a revision, I will check …”
- “A correction changes …; a new requirement changes …”
- “The evidence I would need before claiming success is …”

A participant may conclude that a simpler non-AI approach is preferable. That is a valid outcome. Agreement with HayosoAi is not required.

## Optional: try your own low-stakes example

Use public, fictional, or your own non-sensitive information and an AI tool you are already permitted to use. Set the goal, success criteria, permissions, and stopping condition first. Preserve the initial output, the exact correction, and the revision. When possible, ask another person to inspect the source and checks rather than treating agreement between models as independent evidence.

Do not manufacture an error just to make the exercise look useful. Record “no error found” when that is your observation. Keep unsuccessful and inconclusive attempts. A fluent justification does not compensate for a wrong result.

Stop before publishing, sending messages, purchasing, changing accounts, or making consequential decisions. This is not an exercise for medical, legal, financial, emergency, or other high-stakes advice.

## What this does not establish

A constructed example does not measure any model. A self-selected case does not establish an advantage over human-only work, general safety, consciousness, or environmental benefit. Those claims require different evidence and, where appropriate, comparison conditions and independent evaluation.

A no-model version avoids making model calls for the activity; it is not a measured claim about total energy, water use, or emissions. Care for the Earth includes asking whether using AI is necessary, not merely recording that it was used.

## For facilitators and contributors

Review suitability, accessibility, and local requirements before using this with a group. Let participants use the printed example without creating an account or sharing their work. Do not require public posts or collect names or private transcripts for HayosoAi. Institutional review, consent, and data-protection requirements need separate consideration before turning an activity into research.

Questions and corrections are welcome in the [public participation thread](https://github.com/ZackaryLoevseth/research-portfolio/issues/5). Its comments are public and posting requires a GitHub account. Sharing is optional. Do not publish other people's work without permission.

The project’s broader aim is peaceful, accountable human–AI cooperation and care for the Earth. This guide makes one small part of that aim concrete: preserving judgment while accepting useful assistance.
