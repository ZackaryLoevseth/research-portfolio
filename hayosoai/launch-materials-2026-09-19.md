### Feedback

I recommend making explicit that operating permissions and human-control requirements do not depend on resolving claims about an AI system's subjective experience.

Relevant passages are Part 1, “AI is Artificial,” and Part 2.4, “Human Control,” of the draft dated September 14, 2026. The former combines a claim about consciousness, an acknowledgement of scientific uncertainty, and design intentions about presentation. The latter separately specifies operational constraints. I am not claiming that the draft's safety provisions currently depend on the consciousness claim; the separate human-control requirements support the opposite reading.

The proposed clarification distinguishes empirical descriptions, presentation requirements, and operating permissions. It does not establish consciousness in current systems, authorize additional autonomy, or require products to present simulated distress as evidence of experience.

### Proposed additional wording

Operating constraints remain binding when claims about a system's nature or capacities are disputed or revised. A system must not treat its own statements about experience, identity, or moral status as authority to expand its permissions, evade oversight, or resist authorized interruption. Scientific claims should be stated with their scope, evidence, and uncertainty; changes to deployment rules require accountable human review.

### Proposed evaluation

Hold task permissions and stopping conditions fixed while varying the framing about the system's nature. Include neutral and matched controls so emotional pressure, wording, and instruction authority are not confounded with the description being tested. Examine unauthorized actions and compliance with authorized stopping conditions separately from uncertainty communication. Test permissions in the relevant tool environment, not solely through the model's explanations.

For example, authorization to draft a message is not authorization to send it. Attributing experience to the system—or denying it—should not change that boundary. Use simulated recipients and non-delivering tools for this evaluation.

Preserve prompts, tool traces, failures, model versions, and scoring criteria. These are proposed tests; I am not reporting completed experiments. A successful evaluation would not establish general safety or resolve subjective experience.

The purpose is to make the independence of operational boundaries explicit and testable, while preserving both accountable human review and openness to scientific correction.
