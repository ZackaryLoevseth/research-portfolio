The central question

What must survive
so correction stays possible?

Two histories can lead to the same supported answer while carrying different correction obligations. Preserving the answer alone may discard the distinction needed to respond when a source is withdrawn.

ETI studies task-relevant evidence, support, authority, and correction state. Its broader motivation includes reciprocal criticism in human–AI research; its finite mathematics isolates specified correction decisions.

The framework does not certify that a source is trustworthy or that a constructed record is accurate. Those are separate obligations.

The connected mathematical account

Three regimes. Different guarantees.

Finite common correction

A retained symbol may represent several possible states. Under the complete-interval assumption, a common correction exists exactly when every edit required somewhere is permitted everywhere in that cell.

Required unions, permitted intersections, and conflict graphs characterize one-shot representations. Compatible edit atoms and complete intervals are essential.

§3 · pp. 6–9

Decision-theoretic repair

With unrestricted additive repair loss, optimal action sets have the same interval form at fixed costs. Preserving some optimum differs from reproducing a tie convention.

Preserving optimality for every positive cost vector requires the posterior-impact signature under the stated common-support assumptions.

§5 · pp. 10–14

Sequential maintainability

A representation sufficient for one correction need not be updateable through successive events. The specified fixed-current-state encoding also requires transition congruence.

The model assumes deterministic, exogenous transitions and no free additional history, clock, or reference-state reread.

§§6–7 · pp. 14–17

A concrete support model

Grounded alternative support explains why losing one source need not invalidate a conclusion, while circular references without a grounded entry do not support it.

The illustration uses fixed admitted positive-Horn rules. It is not a general semantics for uncertain or conflicting natural-language evidence.

§4 · pp. 9–10

The synthesis uses established mathematical machinery. Its contribution is the correction-specific formulation, integration, explicit arguments, boundary cases, and evaluation contract—not the invention of graph coloring, conditional expectation, or automaton minimization.

Inspect a clarification / Example 5.3

An optimal decision is not
a tie-breaking convention.

Take two equally likely contexts, one uninformative signal, and one artifact. Impact probabilities are ¼ and ¾. The penalty for leaving an affected artifact active is 1; the penalty for reopening an unaffected artifact is 3.

One common optimum.

Keeping the artifact active is optimal in both contexts. Its average loss is ½, matching the full-information Bayes risk. Zero representation regret does not mean zero loss.

Two codes for a convention.

A “reopen on ties” rule selects different actions at L and H. A representation reproducing that exact rule needs two codes, although the extra code does not improve Bayes risk.

The clarified target is preserving access to some optimal action—not necessarily reproducing a selected canonical policy. This is an example from the manuscript, not a claim about a historical discovery sequence.

Evidence and empirical program

What the manuscript establishes.
What it leaves to test.

Specified arguments and checks.

The manuscript presents proofs under declared assumptions, counterexamples at model boundaries, and reports finite reconstruction checks. It separates those checks from historical execution claims and unrecovered historical validation code.

The website interaction is a new explanatory browser implementation, not a replay of those historical checkers. The manuscript’s companion-package statement is not a promise that every companion file is downloadable here.

Evidence and reproducibility, §9

Construction and use in real tasks.

The empirical question is whether a system can construct, maintain, and use these distinctions from the same permitted sources as competitive alternatives, with acceptable error and cost.

The source edition says Study 14’s competitive retriever and full constructor/scheduler are not frozen. Finite theorem checks do not establish empirical superiority.
