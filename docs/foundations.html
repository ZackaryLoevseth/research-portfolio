Manuscript PDFCorrection example

Problem

Histories supporting the same answer can require different corrections after a source withdrawal. Epistemic Transition Integrity studies which distinctions a retained state must preserve. It does not establish source trustworthiness or record accuracy.

Introduction · pp. 3–4

Mathematical scope

Finite correction

With compatible edit atoms and complete intervals, a representation cell permits a common correction exactly when every required edit is permitted throughout the cell.

§3 · pp. 6–9

Stochastic repair

Unrestricted additive repair loss gives interval-form optima. Preserving optimality for every positive cost vector requires the posterior-impact signature, under stated common-support assumptions.

§5 · pp. 10–14

Sequential revision

Fixed-current-state encodings also require transition congruence: deterministic exogenous transitions, without additional history, a clock, or reference-state rereads.

§§6–7 · pp. 14–17

Grounded support

Fixed admitted positive-Horn rules distinguish alternative support from ungrounded cycles. This is not a general model of uncertain or conflicting natural-language evidence.

§4 · pp. 9–10

The contribution is a correction-specific formulation and integration of established mathematical machinery.
