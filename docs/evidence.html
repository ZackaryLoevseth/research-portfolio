Evidence and corrections

A correction needs a traceable consequence.

Selected examples separate a source-supported correction from an untested repair, and a finite result from a broader scientific claim.

Source identity was not source parity.

The earlier Study 14 check could accept A/B context with matching identifiers and order but changed source text. The successor rejects the changed text. This establishes the tested parity property; it does not validate the whole empirical design.

Boundary witnesses are not always counted vertices.

An earlier unrestricted projection-oracle specification treated a strict-improvement witness as a strict hull vertex. The corrected specification separates valid boundary witnesses from counted strict vertices. This belongs to the earlier parametric-closure engine, not a correction of the bounded semiorder theorem.

A finite pattern failed.

The #413 first-four-offset conjecture failed in a recorded finite check. The exact unpublished counterexample and associated records remain under separate release review. No claim of global held-out secrecy or an infinitude result follows.

Passing tests did not cover every semantic obligation.

Earlier prototype tests missed intended propagation and authority behavior. A passing suite did not establish complete semantic coverage, and the record does not show that every defect has been repaired.

What different evidence can establish.

A written proof supports its stated universal claim. A finite checker exercises its declared domain. A byte hash establishes file identity. A release establishes availability. An AI critic supplies another internal check. External specialist scrutiny, submission and acceptance are separate events.

Previously public mathematical releases provide inspectable arguments and replay materials. Private episode records, correspondence and internal ledgers are excluded from this site.
