Methods

Evidence survives a change of answer.

Explicit assumptions, traceable dependencies, exercised checks and bounded claims make a research object inspectable.

From question to correction.

  1. Write the target, assumptions and stopping conditions.
  2. Separate candidate arguments from established claims.
  3. Inspect load-bearing sources and proof obligations.
  4. Exercise implementations on declared domains and deliberate-error controls.
  5. Reopen claims whose necessary support is lost; retain unaffected or alternatively supported claims.
  6. Release exact objects with limitations and contribution records.

Authorship and material AI assistance.

A separate AI builder and critic are role separation within an AI-assisted process, not external human review.

Project-specific details appear in the secondary contribution panels on Mathematics and Formal systems, and in the frozen papers’ own disclosures.

Scientific claims depend on the arguments, evidence and assumptions. No institutional affiliation, laboratory, team or endorsement is implied.

Reproducibility and reporting.

Keep source versions and expected outputs separate from new work. Record the finite input domain, environment, runtime and exercised controls. Preserve failed checks and explain corrections. A finite case count is not a universal proof, a hash is not correctness, and a public repository is not peer review.

Claims of efficacy require an appropriate empirical comparison. A formal note can be useful before such a comparison when its mathematical scope and practical uncertainty are explicit.
