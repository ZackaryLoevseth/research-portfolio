01 / The handoff

The remembered answer is the same.

Imagine two research records about whether to reopen a candidate route for investigation. At the start, premises p, q, and r are all supported. Both records exclude the route. A deliberately lossy handoff retains only this:

Decision: excluded under recorded grounds. Dependencies mentioned: p, q, r. Original structure of those reasons: not retained.

This is support bookkeeping. “Withdrawn” means we no longer rely on a premise; it does not say the premise is false. These are declared fictional decision rules, not facts about a real model.

Change the evidence available to both records.

Record A

Both p and q, or r alone.

(p AND q) OR r

One sufficient reason needs p and q together. The other needs only r.

Record B

p alone, or both q and r.

p OR (q AND r)

One sufficient reason needs only p. The other needs q and r together.

Reopening is not acceptance. Removing these reasons for rejection does not establish that the candidate works, and does not grant permission for external actions.

02 / The result and its limits

The missing information is the arrangement of reasons.

The flattened summary and update are identical for A and B. An updater with only that information cannot always choose the correct next decision for both. It can instead say that the record is insufficient, or retrieve the original reasons when available.

A faithful prose summary works too. This does not show that JSON is better than language, that a particular AI product fails, or that more memory is always beneficial.

18 of 72

Exactly what was calculated?

Start every rule with all three premises supported. Apply all eight possible support-withdrawal subsets. Retain the original verdict, the unordered union of premise names, and the updated support state—but no rule structure, unique history identifier, source transcript, or external lookup.

The 160 rule–update pairs collapse to 72 distinguishable compressed inputs. Eighteen inputs correspond to histories requiring different decisions. If every pair has equal weight and the updater must choose one of two decisions, at least 24 of the 160 choices must be wrong. That weighting is stipulated, not a measured real-world distribution. Abstention avoids forced-choice mistakes but does not restore the lost information.

The audit independently enumerates Boolean truth tables and reconstructs their sufficient support sets. The earlier probe's self-tests were rerun. Both scripts and their checks are AI-assisted; this is not independent human review.

The next test must be fair.

Compare faithful prose and structured records containing the same information. Separately test what changes when the reasons are omitted. Keep full-history retrieval as a reference, preserve sequential corrections, and report inappropriate reopening as well as missed reopening. The public examples here are development material, not unseen evaluation cases.

03 / The work this builds on

This is not dependency tracking invented from scratch.

Doyle's truth-maintenance work records reasons for beliefs so changes in assumptions can guide revision. LongMemEval includes knowledge updates and abstention. LongMemEval-V2 explicitly includes premise awareness. Letta's memory evaluation separates using existing memory from creating and repairing it.

HayosoAi's candidate contribution is a narrow, checkable case about the grounds for past exclusions across a handoff. Whether it adds a useful evaluation beyond existing work is a question for review—not a settled novelty claim.
